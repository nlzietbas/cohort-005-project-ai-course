# The home page describes the catalog in words, not counts

A visitor said the numbers on the home page made them feel they had to do sums (ticket #3). The home page now names what the catalog holds instead of counting it: the topics tile lists the categories that have at least one published course, in alphabetical order, joined in plain English ("Data Science, Design, and Programming"). It names three at most and ends with "and more" after that, so the tile stays one short phrase in a three-column row. Drafts, archived courses and categories without a published course never show, because a visitor cannot open them.

The counts are not gone: under the tiles a closed "Prefer exact numbers?" disclosure opens to one sentence, "12 published courses in 4 topics", for a visitor who wants the size of the catalog. It stays closed unless the visitor opens it, so nobody meets a number they did not ask for.

The featured course cards follow the same rule: their footer names the course's topic where it used to say "12 lessons".

## Considered Options

- **Keep the counts, reword the labels.** Rejected: the visitor objected to the numbers themselves, not to their labels.
- **Drop the counts entirely.** Rejected: a visitor sizing up the catalog loses the one fact the old tiles gave them, and the ticket objected to being made to read numbers, not to their existing.
- **Name every topic.** Rejected: the list grows with the catalog and stops fitting in its tile.

## Consequences

The tiles no longer say how big the catalog is; only the disclosure does. A visitor who has more than three topics to choose from learns from the tile only that there are more.
